Project 01

Bukkibooks

Okonkwo FinTech Solutions · CS Automation Flow

A scalable Customer Success system that automatically monitors customer health, flags risk, prioritises accounts and triggers the right CSM action, so the team focuses human attention where it matters most.

6step roadmap
11usage signals tracked
3data sources
Health ScoreChurn RiskAutomationHubSpotMixpanelZapierSlack
Read the full case study →

Objectives

  • Build a scalable CS system that monitors customer health and risk, prioritises accounts and triggers actions.
  • Identify the risks and limitations of the system to reduce false alarms and keep risk detection reliable.

Data flow

Bukkibooks app→Mixpanel→Zapier→HubSpot→Slack alert→CS action

What the system reads

  • Product usage (Mixpanel): logins, last activity, active users, feature usage and adoption, frequency, volume, breadth, depth, change over time, and inactivity, with 30- and 90-day rolling averages and deviation.
  • Billing & subscription (HubSpot): plan tier, contract value, renewal date, payments.
  • Support records (HubSpot Help Desk): ticket lifecycle, SLA breaches, response and resolution times, CSAT, FAQ and self-service deflection.

Segmentation & prioritisation

Customers are grouped as healthy, at-risk or high-risk by combining the three signals. Priority follows the severity and combination of those signals, so the CSM's daily HubSpot dashboard shows which accounts need attention first and which can wait.

Roadmap

  • Scalable CS plan → Customer data and health score → Segmentation and prioritisation → Automated risk workflows → CSM intervention → Monitor, review and improve.
Project 02

Sunrise Digital MFB

Customer Experience Review · Board Presentation

A joined-up review of onboarding, customer voice and the full customer journey for a Nigerian digital microfinance bank, presented to the Board as one connected story instead of three separate fixes.

Three connected problems

  • Onboarding loss: only 12% of registrants became active in 30 days. The largest loss by headcount was at BVN/NIN verification (11,350 customers), and the highest-rate loss was between terms acceptance and first deposit (52.99%).
  • Customer frustration: failed transfers, repeated logouts and no way to reach a human agent.
  • Scattered feedback: four channels, no consolidation and no single owner.

Root causes, four customers

No recovery path after a lost verification session, unfamiliar account-tier names, no contact after registering, and no confirmation that documents were received.

Voice of the Customer process

Capture→Classify→Consolidate & theme→Prioritise→Assign & act→Measure & close the loop

Prototype with three views: Feedback Inbox, Theme/Priority Dashboard and Action Tracker.

Moments of truth

  • Onboarding: save-and-resume verification with clear progress and confirmation.
  • First use: transaction status, reference number and resolution time.
  • Ongoing use: acknowledge, own the issue and commit to real follow-up.

Top 3 recommendations

  • Fix transaction reliability and communication.
  • Redesign onboarding around recovery, clarity and confirmation.
  • Establish one owned Voice of the Customer process.

Delivered as a phased roadmap: stabilise transactions (weeks 1–4), redesign onboarding (weeks 3–8), embed VoC (weeks 6–10), then review and improve continuously.

Project 03

Akwete Pay

Merchant Retention, Recovery & Account Growth

An operating plan to win back 312 inactive merchants in a 2,400-merchant market cluster, built around verified root causes rather than discounts, with a priority recovery plan for the largest account.

312inactive merchants
−21%monthly volume

RetentionWin-backAccount GrowthKPIsHubSpot

The situation

Monthly cluster volume fell from ₦6.2bn to ₦4.9bn (−21%). Failed transactions rose from 2% to 7%, and 312 merchants (13%) were inactive for 30 days, 3.25× the normal quarterly level.

What 40 merchants told us

  • 45% cited late settlement.
  • 27.5% cited slow reversals.
  • 17.5% cited a competitor's lower fee or free terminal.
  • 10% gave other reasons, such as a faulty terminal or closed shop.

Operating rule: match the action to the verified cause, and do not lead with discounts when the main issues are service-related.

Priority account: Ifeoma Superstores

Volume fell from ₦48m to ₦17m (−64.6%) with three pending reversals totalling ₦410k. First actions: acknowledge and name one owner, investigate with Operations, assign, communicate realistic updates, confirm with a tested transaction, then retain with follow-ups at day 7 and day 30. Recovery target: 80% of baseline (₦38.4m).

312-merchant recovery playbook

Validate→Prioritise→Contact→Diagnose→Resolve→Reactivate

Two parallel workstreams: operational fixes (settlement, reversals, failure monitoring) and a 90-day merchant recovery process with daily, twice-weekly and weekly review cadences.

Measurement

  • Contact rate, root-cause coverage, case resolution, reactivation, 30-day retention, failure rate and volume recovered.
  • Planning target: test reactivating 25% of the cohort (78 merchants) within 30 days. A target to test, not a forecast.
  • A contact attempt is not a recovery: a first successful transaction is reactivation, and sustained activity is retention.
